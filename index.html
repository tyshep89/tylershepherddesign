My approach to design

Five steps from a messy problem to a measurable result.

01
Ask why

Start with the problem, not the solution.

Everyone arrives with an answer. I slow the room down until we agree on what is actually broken and who feels it.

The question I askWhat problem are we solving, and for whom?

What I watch out forThe first solution anyone suggests.

02
Map the journey

See the whole day, not one screen.

I map how work really flows, including the handoffs and the feelings along the way, because pain hides between the steps.

The question I askWhere does it slow down, and how does that feel?

What I watch out forWorkarounds that people stopped noticing.

03
Reframe

Pick the one thing worth fixing.

There are always ten problems. I look for the one that unlocks the most and say no to the rest, out loud and with reasons.

The question I askIf we fix only one thing, which changes the most?

What I watch out forMistaking a loud problem for an important one.

04
Design small

Make it testable this week.

I sketch, prototype, and put it in front of real people early. A rough idea that teaches me something beats a polished one that does not.

The question I askWhat is the smallest version someone could try?

What I watch out forFalling in love with my own first draft.

05
Prove impact

Tie it back to a result.

A design earns its place when it moves something the business cares about. I decide how we will know it worked before we build.

The question I askHow will we know this worked?

What I watch out forMeasuring activity instead of outcomes.

01The name

It starts with a name

The site is called Tyler Shepherd Design. Three words, and a lot of letters to carry around.

02The initials

Strip it down to TSD

Everything except the initials goes. Three letters are all the mark has to work with, so each one gets its own turn.

03The T

The T is all hard edges

I gave the T the rigid side of the idea: a flat bar, a straight stem, and square corners everywhere.

04Flip the script

Now the same T is all curves

Rotate it and the rules reverse. The bar becomes a soft arch and the stem bends away like a brush stroke.

05The S

The S is all curves

The S gets the opposite treatment to start: one smooth line with no corners at all.

06Flip the script

Turn it and it turns hard

Rotate the S and it becomes a folded ribbon of straight lines and sharp corners.

07The D

The D balances both

A D already holds both ideas: a straight edge and a curve. Pull it apart, then turn the curve on its side and let it stretch into the sweep of the logo.

08Putting it together

The T and S come over the D

The straight edge steps aside because the bottom of the S takes its place. The T and the S come over the curve and balance on it.

09The mark

Merge it into one shape

Trace it, join the strokes, and the three letters become a single mark: curves and hard lines holding each other up.

10One more thing

Squint real hard

If you squint real hard, it kinda looks like a face.

11Squint harder

Squint harder

And if you squint harder, it even kinda looks like my face.
